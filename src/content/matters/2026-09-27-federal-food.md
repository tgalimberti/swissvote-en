<h2>In brief</h2>
<h1>Popular initiative<br>«For a secure food supply – through the strengthening of a sustainable national production, more plant-based foodstuffs and clean drinking water<br>(Food Initiative)»</h1>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Context</div>
<div>
The Swiss population must be able to have at all times a sufficient quantity of high-quality foodstuffs at accessible prices. The Confederation must create the appropriate framework conditions. Currently, Swiss agriculture covers slightly less than half of the foodstuff needs. To protect the environment, biodiversity and drinking water, Swiss agriculture will have to respect a whole series of constraints.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">The initiative</div>
<div>
The initiative asks that Switzerland produce a significantly greater quantity of foodstuffs in order to reduce its dependence on abroad. In the future, at least 70 per cent of the foodstuffs consumed in our Country will have to be of domestic production. With this objective, with the same surface area, it will be possible to feed a greater number of people. The initiative also aims to improve the protection of the environment and of drinking water as well as the preservation of soil fertility and biodiversity. Farmers will have to use fewer plant protection products and fertilizers. The objectives will have to be achieved within ten years of the acceptance of the initiative.
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">The question that appears on the ballot</div>
<div style="font-size: 1.3em; font-weight: bold; color: var(--swiss-red);">
Do you wish to accept the popular initiative «For a secure food supply – through the strengthening of a sustainable national production, more plant-based foodstuffs and clean drinking water (Food Initiative)»?
</div>
</div>

<div class="recommendation-box">
<div class="side-label">Recommendation of the Federal Council and Parliament</div>
<div>
<div class="big-rec">No</div>
<p>For the Federal Council and Parliament the requests of the initiative are excessive. Its objectives are practically impossible to achieve within ten years. The State would have to intervene massively in agricultural production and in eating habits. This would entail high costs and would limit the freedom of choice of consumers.</p>
<a href="https://admin.ch/iniziativa-alimentazione" class="url-link" target="_blank">admin.ch/iniziativa-alimentazione</a>
</div>
</div>

<div class="recommendation-box">
<div class="side-label">Recommendation of the initiative committee</div>
<div>
<div class="big-rec">Yes</div>
<p>According to the committee, Swiss agriculture alone is currently not able to feed the population in case of a crisis; moreover, drinking water is not sufficiently protected. The initiative therefore aims to strengthen the supply of foodstuffs of domestic production, especially plant-based ones, and of clean drinking water.</p>
<a href="https://iniziativa-per-un-alimentazione-sicura.ch" class="url-link" target="_blank">iniziativa-per-un-alimentazione-sicura.ch</a>

<div class="section-box">
<h2 style="border:none; margin-top:0;">In detail</h2>
<h1 style="border:none; margin-bottom: 0;">Popular initiative<br>«For a secure food supply – through the strengthening of a sustainable national production, more plant-based foodstuffs and clean drinking water<br>(Food Initiative)»</h1>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Context<br><br>Food supply</div>
<div>
Swiss agriculture today produces slightly less than half of the foodstuffs consumed in the Country. The rest comes from abroad; to guarantee the supply, Switzerland depends on imports. This also applies to the means of production used to obtain foodstuffs in Switzerland, such as for example fertilizers and seeds.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Environmental protection in agriculture</div>
<div>
Swiss agriculture must respect a whole series of constraints to protect the environment, biodiversity and water. In recent years, progress has been made in the increasingly sparing use of plant protection products and fertilizers. For example, today plant protection products and fertilizers are used only where necessary.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Evolution of agricultural policy</div>
<div>
Despite the progress, the Federal Council and Parliament consider that it is still necessary to intervene. Within the framework of the evolution of agricultural policy, it is necessary to provide for measures aimed at increasing food security and at making agriculture and the food chain more ecological. In February 2026 the Federal Council communicated the guidelines of the future agricultural policy and instructed the Administration to elaborate the related draft to be put into consultation within the third quarter of 2026<sup>1</sup>.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">The requests of the initiative</div>
<div>
The food initiative asks that our agriculture produce a significantly greater share of the foodstuffs consumed in Switzerland. To be able to feed a higher number of people on the same surface area, it is necessary to produce and consume less food of animal origin. The initiative also asks that drinking water be expressly included among the foodstuffs to be guaranteed. What is requested by the initiative will have to be implemented within ten years of its acceptance.
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Strengthening self-sufficiency</div>
<div>
Currently Switzerland covers slightly less than half of its own foodstuff needs. The so-called net self-sufficiency rate (see box below) averaged 45 per cent in the years 2022–2024<sup>2</sup>. The initiative committee asks that it be increased to at least 70 per cent. This would reduce dependence on abroad and would allow Switzerland to provide for its own supply in case of a crisis. The Federal Council also considers the increase of the net self-sufficiency rate a priority and plans to bring it to at least 50 per cent by 2050<sup>3</sup>.
</div>
</div>

<div class="info-box">
<div class="info-box-title">Self-sufficiency rate</div>
<p>The self-sufficiency rate – expressed in calories – indicates the share of foodstuffs consumed in Switzerland that is produced within the Country. A distinction is made between the gross self-sufficiency rate and the net self-sufficiency rate. The latter takes into account the part of the production obtained using imported fodder. To calculate the net self-sufficiency rate, the share obtained using imported fodder is subtracted from the share of foodstuffs of animal origin produced in Switzerland.</p>
<p>The self-sufficiency rate is an important indicator of the capacity of a Country to withstand crises or difficulties of supply at the international level. The self-sufficiency rate does not, however, allow establishing whether the population effectively has access to a sufficient quantity of high-quality foodstuffs at accessible prices. Nor does it indicate to what extent Swiss agriculture depends on important means of production such as, for example, fertilizers, plant protection products or seeds. The latter are often imported from abroad.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Share of domestic foodstuff production relative to total consumption (net self-sufficiency rate*)</div>
<div>
Swiss agriculture today produces slightly less than half of the foodstuffs consumed in Switzerland. If the initiative is accepted, this share will have to reach 70 per cent within 10 years. The agricultural policy of the Federal Council plans to increase this share to at least 50 per cent by 2050.
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">More plant-based foodstuffs</div>
<div>
The initiative aims to orient Swiss agriculture and the food chain more strongly towards the production and consumption of plant foods. Currently, in Switzerland the production of foodstuffs of animal origin (meat, milk, eggs) is of great importance. This is due, among other things, to the fact that around 60 per cent of the useful agricultural area in our Country is not suitable for cultivation. For the purposes of foodstuff production, these surfaces can be used only as pastures for cows, sheep or goats. In the mountain region there are thus exclusively grassland soils. Moreover, more than half of the arable land is used for the production of animal feed, for example fodder cereals for pigs.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Preserving ecosystems and biodiversity</div>
<div>
The initiative wants to preserve soil fertility and biodiversity, that is the variety of animals and plants and their habitats. Biodiversity and soil fertility are fundamental preconditions for high-yield agriculture. The environmental objectives for agriculture have established maximum values for environmental impact since 2008. Not all of these objectives have been achieved. The initiative therefore asks for greater protection of ecosystems. Farmers will have to use plant protection products and fertilizers more sparingly, so as to reduce the impact on the environment.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Guaranteeing clean drinking water</div>
<div>
Drinking water in Switzerland is of good quality and is constantly monitored. Around 80 per cent comes from groundwater and springs, while around 20 per cent comes from lakes. Groundwater can be contaminated by residues of plant protection products and fertilizers. The initiative aims to protect it better, so that clean drinking water is possible also in the future<sup>4</sup>.
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Consequences of the initiative</div>
<div>
If the initiative were accepted, Switzerland would depend to a lesser extent on imports of foodstuffs in case of a crisis. The environmental objectives for agriculture set by the Federal Council could be achieved. However, within ten years agriculture and the food chain would have to orient themselves strongly towards plant production, while that of foodstuffs of animal origin would have to decrease. The population would have to change its eating habits. If this were not to happen and many foods of animal origin continued to be consumed, it would be necessary to resort to imports to an increasing extent. The negative effects on the environment would therefore be transferred abroad.
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="argument-container">
<h2 style="border:none; margin-bottom: 0;">Arguments</h2>

<!-- Initiative Committee (PRO) -->
<div class="arg-title pro">Initiative Committee</div>
<p style="font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 25px; font-size: 1.1em;">Our food supply depends for 58 per cent on abroad. Despite the 3.6 billion francs of annual subsidies, agriculture is not structured to feed the population by drawing on its own resources in case of interruptions of imports or of shortages. The supply of drinking water, moreover, is not sufficiently protected from pollution and from the consequences of climate change. This is where the initiative intervenes, guaranteeing a crisis-proof supply of indigenous foodstuffs and clean drinking water.</p>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Self-sufficiency means security</div>
<div>
Food security is enshrined in the Constitution; however, our self-sufficiency is in constant decline. In case of a crisis, agriculture is not structured to feed the population with its own resources alone, although the law guarantees a sufficient agricultural area for this purpose. The initiative obliges the Confederation to aim for a net self-sufficiency rate of 70 per cent and to promote plant-based foodstuffs more strongly. To this end, the cultivation of plant foods should be increased in place of fodder maize, while pastures should continue to be used for the production of milk and meat. Today, 75 per cent of the 3.6 billion francs of agricultural subsidies is destined to animal production, which heavily penalizes the production and consumption of plant-based foodstuffs. As a consequence, 65 per cent of the plant-based foodstuffs we consume is imported.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Fertile soils, secure harvests</div>
<div>
The initiative asks to safeguard soil fertility and biodiversity, essential preconditions for having secure and abundant harvests and for protecting crops in a natural way. The over-fertilization with liquid manure, manure and ammonia resulting from an animal production boosted through imported feed, however, causes the progressive impoverishment of the soil and of biodiversity. This leads to the exceeding of the maximum limits regarding the use of fertilizers, provided for by the environmental objectives for agriculture for the protection of the soil and of the waters since 2008. The initiative obliges the Confederation to guarantee the respect of such limits.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">The water supply is not assured</div>
<div>
Climate change and pollution threaten our water resources. The initiative asks to guarantee the availability of clean drinking water by preserving the aquifers necessary for this purpose. A national planning of the water supply is therefore indispensable, on which agriculture also depends. Food security is fundamental for our life and for the security of our Country. Thanks to the initiative, the billions destined to agricultural subsidies will be invested to guarantee a supply of indigenous foodstuffs and drinking water also in case of a crisis. Moreover, farmers will benefit from a secure production and from new markets.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point" style="margin-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color); padding-top: 20px;">
<div class="arg-point-title">Recommendation of the initiative committee</div>
<div>
For all these reasons the initiative committee recommends voting

</div>
</div>

<!-- Divider between PRO and CON -->
<div style="margin-top: 60px; padding-top: 40px; border-top: 2px dashed var(--border-color);">
<!-- Federal Council and Parliament (CON) -->
<div class="arg-title con">Federal Council and Parliament</div>
<p style="font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 25px; font-size: 1.1em;">The Federal Council recognizes that Swiss agriculture must contribute in a significant way to food security and, at the same time, become even more ecological. However, the food initiative is excessive and sets objectives too ambitious to be achieved in too short a time. The State would have to intervene massively in production and in eating habits. This would entail high costs and would limit the freedom of choice of consumers. The Federal Council and Parliament reject the initiative in particular for the reasons set out below.</p>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">The need to proceed gradually</div>
<div>
The Federal Council shares certain concerns at the basis of the initiative. It is already developing its agricultural policy in the direction requested by the initiative. It intends to increase domestic production and to further reduce the ecological footprint of agriculture and of the food chain. Unlike the initiative, however, the agricultural policy of the Federal Council relies on gradual adjustments, which grant agriculture and the food chain the time necessary for the implementation.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">An unrealistic objective</div>
<div>
It is unrealistic to think of increasing the net self-sufficiency rate to 70 per cent in ten years. To achieve such an objective, profound changes would be necessary in agricultural production, in the transformation of foodstuffs and in the eating habits of the Swiss population. It would have to consume a significantly greater quantity of plant foods and reduce the consumption of those of animal origin, such as meat, milk and eggs.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Difficult to implement</div>
<div>
The initiative goes in the right direction, but its requests are excessive and set objectives too ambitious to be achieved in too short a time. To realize radical changes in the little time provided for by the initiative, it would be necessary to intervene massively in the production and in the consumption of foodstuffs through binding prescriptions. The short period provided for the implementation of the initiative would not allow farmers and the sectors concerned to fully amortize the investments already made, for example for the stables. The initiative therefore provides for supplementary financial support from the Confederation so that the transition can be implemented, as requested, in a socially sustainable way.

Depending on how the measures are structured, this could entail high costs for the Confederation. Within the framework of the evolution of agricultural policy, the Federal Council and Parliament are already addressing the legitimate requests of the initiative, such as the strengthening of food security and of the protection of the environment in agriculture.

For all these reasons, the Federal Council and Parliament recommend rejecting the food initiative.
