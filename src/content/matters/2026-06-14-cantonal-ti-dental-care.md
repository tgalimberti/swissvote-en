<h2>Subject of the vote</h2>

<p>The generic legislative popular initiative "For the reimbursement of dental care" proposes creating a mandatory cantonal insurance to guarantee coverage for basic dental care expenses. This insurance would be financed through an additional contribution paid by employees and employers, as well as a contribution from the Canton.</p>

<p>Today, the Federal Health Insurance Act (LAMal) reimburses dental care only in specific cases, such as serious diseases of the masticatory system or specific systemic pathologies. Furthermore, the Cantons directly assume the costs of prophylaxis and dental care for the most economically fragile segments of the population. Overall, however, over 90% of costs are borne by patients, either directly or through private insurance coverage. The initiative in question would place these costs on a mandatory insurance, the financing of which would have to be guaranteed. The modalities of this financing will eventually be defined at a later stage.</p>

<p>Similar proposals have also been presented in some cantons in French-speaking Switzerland, after the Federal Parliament refused to extend health insurance coverage to dental care, both because of the significant increase in health insurance premiums that would have resulted, and in consideration of the notable improvement in the population's dental health over recent decades. In the cantons where the population has voted on similar initiatives — Vaud, Geneva, and Neuchâtel — the proposals were rejected at the ballot box.</p>

<p>Having been presented in a generic form, the Grand Council deemed it appropriate to codify the initiative through a constitutional norm. This method allows the principle of establishing a mandatory dental care insurance to be put to a vote, while avoiding preliminary definitions of concepts such as the notion of basic dental care, the proportion between public financing and additional contributions on salaries, or any contribution rates for employees and employers.</p>
</div>

<h2 style="border:none; margin-bottom: 0;">Arguments</h2>

<!-- Initiative Promoters (PRO) -->
<div class="arg-title pro">Why vote YES</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Guarantee everyone the possibility of treatment</div>
<div>
The initiative represents an important step in tackling the inequalities, increasingly marked in these times of economic crisis, in accessing dental care in Ticino. A substantial part of the population cannot afford adequate care due to costs. There are those who give it up completely and those who delay it waiting for better times. Existing subsidies (for beneficiaries of assistance or supplementary benefits) are not enough, because they exclude the middle brackets of the population, which are those hit hardest by the increase in health insurance premiums. Some patients go abroad to save money, facing risks linked particularly to the discontinuity of care. The initiative aims to guarantee equitable access in Ticino to basic dental services, which are necessary to maintain dental health.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">From dental care to general health</div>
<div>
All scientific studies show the link between oral health and general health. Untreated periodontal diseases and dental infections are associated with various pathologies, including diabetes, infections, complications during pregnancy, and cardiovascular diseases. Health complications become particularly severe in the elderly, who are frequently affected by dental health disorders and are more fragile due to other pathologies. The initiative allows for improved prevention by identifying important risks early, thus benefiting public health and reducing the costs generated by late and more complex care.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Concrete support for the population and families</div>
<div>
Today we have "two-speed dentistry": those who have resources can access modern care, while those who do not are forced to give it up or to get treated poorly and late. Dental care represents the most significant part of a household's health costs, often exceeding 20%. Reducing these costs means helping the population, effectively supporting even those who remain excluded from both social aid and the possibility of affording supplementary insurance. This is an objective to be pursued also by strengthening the preventative measures regarding oral health and the network of public dental clinics.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">A public, transparent and sustainable insurance</div>
<div>
The initiative plans to guarantee the reimbursement of dental care through the establishment of a public insurance. The financing model is inspired by the proven AHV/AVS model: contributions proportional to income, divided equally between workers and employers, with a state contribution for those not active in the workforce. This means that, regardless of income, everyone can access basic services. The exact contribution rate, the catalogue of covered services, and any cost-sharing modalities will be defined by the legislator during the implementation phase, guaranteeing flexibility and adaptability to the Canton's economic situation, as well as the possibility to consult with sector technicians to define a catalogue of services that allows access to adequate care. More and more Ticino residents struggle to face unexpected expenses. The creation of this "public health fund" for dental care, financed proportionally to income, would bring savings, relieving the population from a major economic concern.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Reimbursing dental care: a necessary principle</div>
<div>
The introduction of a public insurance for basic dental care is a sustainable reform, socially necessary, and extremely sensible from a public health perspective. The initiative asks to establish a fundamental principle in the Cantonal Constitution: every person residing in Ticino has the right to the reimbursement of basic dental care, guaranteed by a public insurance. The precise model, rates, and list of services will be determined in the subsequent legislative phase, taking into account both resources and technical needs. This is a path already known in Swiss institutional history. Maternity insurance was also born this way: first the principle of its necessity was established in the Federal Constitution, then Parliament drafted the implementation law. The initiative asks to finally recognize that dental health is part of general health and, as such, is a right that must be accessible to all.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point" style="margin-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color); padding-top: 20px;">
<div class="arg-point-title">Recommendation of the promoters</div>
<div>
For all these reasons, the promoters of the initiative recommend voting <strong style="color: var(--swiss-red);">YES</strong> to the generic legislative popular initiative.
</div>
</div>

<!-- Grand Council (CON) -->
<div class="arg-title con">Why vote NO</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Current successful system for dental care</div>
<div>
In Switzerland, the dental care system is founded on three basic pillars: prevention, individual responsibility, and the free choice of dentist. This model, thanks to the population's increased attention to oral hygiene and prevention measures, has favored a marked improvement in dental health across all age groups since the 1960s. In particular, it is proven that cavities in children and adolescents have strongly decreased, in the order of 80-90%. Even in international comparisons, the results of the Swiss model are considered very positive.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Very effective prevention, especially at school age</div>
<div>
A central and successful element of the Swiss model is represented by the prevention and dental care programs in schools and by the widespread use of fluoridation measures aimed at the entire population. In Ticino, the School Dental Service, to which about 70% of elementary and middle school students adhere, allows over 20,000 young and very young people every year to benefit from dental prophylaxis measures and check-ups, focusing on prevention from a young age with contained average costs.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Dental expenses already financed for those in need</div>
<div>
The Canton supports the most economically fragile people in paying for dental care, in particular by assuming the costs for beneficiaries of supplementary benefits to the AHV/IV and social assistance, according to criteria of simplicity, adequacy, and affordability. For these segments of the population, establishing a mandatory dental insurance would therefore not bring substantial changes. Conversely, having to guarantee adequate financing, it would generalize the coverage of expenses regardless of income, disregarding the principle of targeted social aid calibrated to need.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Overall costs for dental health contained and under control</div>
<div>
Individual responsibility and good dental health keep overall costs relatively low. In 2023, out of total health expenditure in Switzerland amounting to 94 billion francs, only 4 billion were attributable to dental practices and clinics, which is 4.2% of the total. The evolution of costs confirms this trend: in the last decade, dental care costs have grown overall by around 4%, while total health expenditure and health insurance premiums have increased, nationally, by over 30%.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Financially unsustainable impact</div>
<div>
Even if a precise estimate depends on the definition of the services that would be covered, the costs of a mandatory cantonal dental care insurance could reach up to 150 million francs per year. This amount would have to be financed with new contributions from employees, employers, and cantonal finances. The contribution could reach a total of up to 0.8% of salary. An impact of this magnitude is unsustainable, especially following the approval, last September, of the popular initiatives to alleviate the burden of health insurance premiums on citizens. These initiatives already bring an additional burden to cantonal finances in the order of hundreds of millions of francs.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Bureaucratic complexity</div>
<div>
The introduction of a mandatory insurance would require a complex public administrative structure to guarantee the quality, relevance, and cost control of services, generating further expenses and increasing bureaucracy.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Renouncing care: a limited phenomenon</div>
<div>
With the current system, largely based on individual resources, the phenomenon of renouncing care for financial reasons remains contained. Indeed, according to the most recent data from the Federal Statistical Office, only 5% of the population in Switzerland declares they renounce necessary dental care for economic reasons.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Disincentive to prevention</div>
<div>
Similar initiatives, rejected at the federal level and in other Cantons, along with economic studies indicate that a mandatory dental insurance offers limited advantages, does not eliminate inequalities, and can reduce the incentives for prevention and individual responsibility that characterize the Swiss dental care system.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point" style="margin-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color); padding-top: 20px;">
<div class="arg-point-title">Recommendation of the Grand Council</div>
<div>
For all these reasons, it is recommended to vote <strong style="color: var(--swiss-red);">NO</strong> to the generic legislative popular initiative.
</div>
</div>
</div>
