<h2>In brief</h2>
<h1>Popular initiative<br>«Safeguarding Swiss neutrality<br>(Neutrality Initiative)»</h1>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Context</div>
<div>
Enshrined in the Federal Constitution since 1848, Switzerland's neutrality is recognized at the international level and guaranteed by international law. Switzerland uses it to protect its security, its independence and its prosperity. It is also thanks to its neutrality that Switzerland can offer its good offices. In conformity with its neutrality, Switzerland cooperates with other States to strengthen its own security. It can also adopt sanctions, thus supporting the international legal order. For example, our Country adopted most of the sanctions decreed by the EU against Russia following the aggression against Ukraine, which took place in violation of international law.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">The initiative</div>
<div>
The initiative asks to adopt a stricter practice regarding neutrality and to enshrine in the Constitution that Switzerland's neutrality is permanent and armed. According to the text of the initiative, Switzerland must not join military or defensive alliances and must not cooperate with them, except in the case of direct military aggression or of preparatory acts in view of such an aggression. Moreover, the initiative provides that our Country does not participate in military clashes between third-party States and does not adopt sanctions against belligerent States, with the exception of those of the United Nations (UN) and of the measures intended to prevent the evasion of sanctions. Finally, it asks that Switzerland make use of its neutrality to offer its good offices as a mediator.
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">The question that appears on the ballot</div>
<div style="font-size: 1.3em; font-weight: bold; color: var(--swiss-red);">
Do you wish to accept the popular initiative «Safeguarding Swiss neutrality (Neutrality Initiative)»?
</div>
</div>

<div class="recommendation-box">
<div class="side-label">Recommendation of the Federal Council and Parliament</div>
<div>
<div class="big-rec">No</div>
<p>The Federal Council and Parliament are of the opinion that the practice followed in matters of neutrality since 1848 has produced good results. The Federal Constitution today guarantees the necessary room for maneuver so that, in compliance with the law of neutrality, the Federal Council can protect Switzerland's interests even as circumstances change. The initiative would limit this room for maneuver.</p>

<div class="section-box">
<h2 style="border:none; margin-top:0;">In detail</h2>
<h1 style="border:none; margin-bottom: 0;">Popular initiative<br>«Safeguarding Swiss neutrality<br>(Neutrality Initiative)»</h1>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Context<br><br>Permanent and armed neutrality</div>
<div>
Developed over the course of several centuries, Swiss neutrality has been enshrined in international law since 1815 and in the Federal Constitution since 1848. Switzerland's neutrality is permanent and armed, which means that Switzerland remains neutral in all wars between States and the army defends the Country and its neutrality.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Neutrality in the Constitution</div>
<div>
The Federal Constitution provides that the Federal Council and Parliament adopt measures for the protection of Switzerland's neutrality. The Executive and the Legislature make use of neutrality to safeguard the interests of the Country, in particular to protect security, independence and prosperity.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Neutrality in international law</div>
<div>
The law of neutrality is part of international law. Switzerland's neutrality was recognized at the Congress of Vienna and enshrined in the Peace of Paris of 1815. Since then, from the 19th century, States have developed a framework of practice that was later codified in the Hague Conventions of 1907. These establish how a neutral State must behave towards a belligerent State. In particular, a neutral State cannot participate in armed clashes between other States, must not provide military support to the belligerent parties and must not place its own territory at the disposal of the latter. Within the framework set by these rules, there is a certain room for maneuver in applying the principle of neutrality.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Aggression against a neutral State</div>
<div>
If attacked, the neutral State is not bound by the law of neutrality; it therefore has the right to defend itself and to conclude alliances with other States.
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Neutrality in practice</div>
<div>
Since the foundation of the federal State in 1848, the Federal Council and Parliament have made use of neutrality to safeguard Switzerland's interests in foreign, security and economic policy. This practice adheres to international law and guarantees Switzerland the room for maneuver it needs to protect the interests of the Country in different foreign-policy contexts. As periodic polls show, neutrality enjoys broad consensus among Swiss citizens.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Neutrality and good offices</div>
<div>
Thanks to its neutrality, Switzerland is often called upon to mediate in international conflicts and to make its good offices available. An example of this are the international talks regularly held in Geneva.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Neutrality and the war against Ukraine</div>
<div>
With Russia's aggression against Ukraine in violation of international law, the security situation in Europe has deteriorated. Switzerland reacted to this aggravation by strengthening security cooperation with its partners and by adopting a large part of the sanctions imposed by the EU against Russia. These sanctions are compatible with Switzerland's neutrality and aim to guarantee respect for international law. Switzerland decides autonomously which sanctions to adopt and which not.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">The criticisms of adopting sanctions</div>
<div>
The promoters of the initiative are of the opinion that, by adopting the EU sanctions, Switzerland did not strictly adhere to neutrality and thus became a belligerent party. For this reason they submitted the initiative<sup>1</sup>.
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">What does the initiative ask for?</div>
<div>
The initiative provides for enshrining in the Constitution an article that defines neutrality in a more restrictive way. The article in question establishes that:
<ul>
<li>Switzerland's neutrality is permanent and armed;</li>
<li>Switzerland cannot join military or defensive alliances;</li>
<li>cooperation with such alliances is allowed only in the case of direct military aggression against Switzerland or in view of such an aggression;</li>
<li>Switzerland cannot participate in military clashes between third-party States;</li>
<li>Switzerland cannot adopt sanctions against belligerent States, with the exception of UN sanctions;</li>
<li>Switzerland can adopt measures intended to prevent the evasion of sanctions adopted by other States;</li>
<li>Switzerland makes use of its neutrality to offer its good offices as a mediator.</li>
</ul>
<p>Many of these requests already coincide with the legal obligations of a neutral State and are therefore already satisfied in the current practice of our Country:</p>
<ul>
<li>Switzerland's neutrality is already permanent and armed; our Country is not part of any military or defensive alliance;</li>
<li>already today Switzerland does not take part in military clashes between States;</li>
<li>Switzerland already adopts all UN sanctions, as it is bound to do as a member State;</li>
<li>Switzerland already implements the request of the promoting committee to make use of its own neutrality to offer its good offices as a mediator and to promote peace.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Consequences of the initiative</div>
<div>
Introducing a restrictive definition of neutrality into the Constitution, as the initiative asks, would limit Switzerland's room for maneuver.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Reduced defense capabilities</div>
<div>
To strengthen its own defense capabilities, Switzerland cooperates with selected States, in particular with its neighboring States. This cooperation comprises the purchase of armaments, the exchange of information and joint exercises. Switzerland adheres to the law of neutrality and does not join any alliance that entails an obligation of mutual assistance. The initiative intends to further limit this cooperation, allowing it only in the case of direct military aggression or of preparatory acts in view of such an aggression. This would have serious consequences for the security of our Country: Switzerland would be deprived of a precious exchange of experience and would be considered less of a reliable partner in international security. Moreover, to defend itself Switzerland must be able to rely on the solidity of its own arms industry; the initiative would damage the national technological and armaments sector and would compromise trust in the armament companies of our Country.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">No possibility of choice in matters of sanctions</div>
<div>
The initiative also intends to prohibit Switzerland from adopting international sanctions against belligerent States, with the exception of those of the UN. For example, Switzerland could no longer apply the sanctions ordered by the EU against Russia following the aggression against Ukraine and could even be required to revoke the sanctions already in force. Today the Federal Council decides case by case whether to adopt sanctions, assessing their effectiveness and taking into account foreign-policy, economic and legal considerations.
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="argument-container">
<h2 style="border:none; margin-bottom: 0;">Arguments</h2>

<!-- Initiative Committee (PRO) -->
<div class="arg-title pro">Initiative Committee</div>
<p style="font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 25px; font-size: 1.1em;">For centuries, Swiss neutrality has preserved our Country from the sufferings of war, guaranteeing us peace, security and well-being. Neutrality can, however, protect us only if it remains credible and reliable. The initiative guarantees that Switzerland durably safeguards its own neutrality, unique of its kind, armed and permanent, without interpreting it in a «flexible» way under external pressure.</p>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Neutrality or war</div>
<div>
Neutrality has preserved Switzerland from the horrors of war for generations. Being neutral means not taking sides with any of the parties at war – and it is precisely this that allows us not to be dragged into foreign conflicts. A credible, armed and total neutrality generates security and deters aggressors.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Not case by case</div>
<div>
Switzerland is reliable because its neutrality is not selective, but rather permanent; and it must manifest itself towards every war or conflict. Our Country must not join any military or defensive alliance. The dangerous rapprochement to the structures of the EU and of NATO would lead Switzerland towards military dependencies and conflicts. It is important that the State, the Government and Parliament remain strictly neutral. This does not, however, concern the citizens: everyone can indeed express their own ideas, since neutrality protects the freedom of opinion.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Non-military coercive measures kill</div>
<div>
Total neutrality entails the renunciation of coercive measures of an economic kind. Sanctions rarely punish those directly responsible while they often hit the civilian population, in particular women and children, causing hunger, misery and death. A neutral and humanitarian Switzerland must not adhere to measures that hit the innocent. Neutrality is the protective shield that has made our Country strong, making neutral Switzerland, open to the world, a reliable counterpart both for people who aspire to peace and for business partners who seek stability.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point" style="margin-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color); padding-top: 20px;">
<div class="arg-point-title">Neutrality promotes peace</div>
<div>
Switzerland continues to fulfil its obligations towards the UN and uses its neutrality to prevent conflicts and promote peace. It provides humanitarian aid to all affected people. The world situation is worsening and now more than ever our Country must remain faithful to its proven neutrality. The question is the following: do we want Swiss neutrality or war in Switzerland?
<p>For all these reasons the initiative committee recommends voting</p>

<!-- Divider between PRO and CON -->
<div style="margin-top: 60px; padding-top: 40px; border-top: 2px dashed var(--border-color);">
<!-- Federal Council and Parliament (CON) -->
<div class="arg-title con">Federal Council and Parliament</div>
<p style="font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 25px; font-size: 1.1em;">Neutrality has been enshrined in the Constitution since 1848. The Federal Council and Parliament recognize its great value. The practice followed so far guarantees Switzerland the room for maneuver it needs to protect the security, the independence and the prosperity of the Country. If accepted, the initiative would, however, strongly limit such room for maneuver. The Federal Council and Parliament reject the initiative in particular for the reasons set out below.</p>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">A room for maneuver in the interest of the Country</div>
<div>
Switzerland is and remains neutral. It applies neutrality in a flexible way, according to the interests of the Country. The room for maneuver necessary for this purpose is, however, not absolute, since international law and domestic law establish its limits. The initiative, instead, intends to enshrine in the Constitution a rigid definition of neutrality, thus limiting Switzerland's options and hindering the protection of the interests of the Country as it has happened so far.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Switzerland's security at risk</div>
<div>
If accepted, the initiative would hinder security cooperation. Such cooperation must in fact be prepared in times of peace: it cannot begin only after Switzerland has been attacked, as this initiative provides. Switzerland would be considered less of a reliable partner in international security and, in the case of an aggression, would be less protected.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Weakening of the arms industry</div>
<div>
To defend itself, Switzerland must be able to rely on the solidity of its own arms industry. The initiative would therefore also damage the national technological and armaments sector. The initiative would compromise trust in the armament companies of our Country; Switzerland would no longer be considered a reliable partner in matters of armaments.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">A change of course in sanctions policy</div>
<div>
Switzerland does not pursue a policy of power and works in favour of respect for international law. Sanctions are an important instrument used by States to respond to violations of international law. As a small Country, Switzerland benefits in a particular way from this order. If the initiative were accepted, Switzerland could no longer adopt numerous sanctions pronounced against belligerent States. This would have repercussions on the interests and on the reputation of Switzerland and in some cases could provoke negative reactions from the other States.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">An unnecessary initiative</div>
<div>
The initiative intends to enshrine a practice that is partly already applied. Where the initiative goes beyond current practices, as in the case of the policy on security and sanctions, it harms the interests of the Country.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point" style="margin-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color); padding-top: 20px;">
<div class="arg-point-title">Recommendation of the Federal Council and Parliament</div>
<div>
For all these reasons, the Federal Council and Parliament recommend rejecting the popular initiative «Safeguarding Swiss neutrality (Neutrality Initiative)».
