<h2>In brief</h2>
<h1>Popular initiative<br>«No to a 10 million Switzerland!<br>(Sustainability initiative)»</h1>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Context</div>
<div>
At the end of 2025, Switzerland had approximately 9.1 million inhabitants. Since the introduction of the free movement of persons in 2002, the population has increased by about 1.7 million people. The increase is mainly due to immigration. The number of immigrants depends primarily on the labor market. In times of economic prosperity, companies cannot find enough personnel in Switzerland. Companies, but also public structures such as hospitals and nursing homes, often turn to the European Union area to find the missing specialized labor.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">The initiative</div>
<div>
The initiative «No to a 10 million Switzerland! (Sustainability initiative)» asks to limit the permanent resident population in our country, establishing that it cannot exceed ten million inhabitants before 2050. If the permanent resident population exceeds 9.5 million inhabitants before 2050, the Federal Council and Parliament would have to adopt measures, particularly in the asylum and family reunification sectors. The Federal Council would also have to invoke the exception or safeguard clauses provided for by international agreements that contribute to demographic growth or negotiate new ones. If the limit of ten million people is exceeded, Switzerland would have to denounce these agreements and, after two years, also the Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons concluded with the European Union. In this case, the other Bilateral I Agreements would also cease to apply. Switzerland's participation in the Schengen and Dublin agreements of the European Union would also be jeopardized, and with it, close cooperation in the security and asylum sectors.
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">The question that appears on the ballot</div>
<div style="font-size: 1.3em; font-weight: bold; color: var(--swiss-red);">
Do you wish to accept the popular initiative «No to a 10 million Switzerland! (Sustainability initiative)»?
</div>
</div>

<div class="section-box">
<h2 style="border:none; margin-top:0;">In detail</h2>
<h1 style="border:none; margin-bottom: 0;">Popular initiative<br>«No to a 10 million Switzerland!<br>(Sustainability initiative)»</h1>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Context<br><br>Demographic evolution</div>
<div>
At the end of 2025, just over 9.1 million people lived permanently in Switzerland. Since the introduction of the free movement of persons in 2002, the population has grown by about 1.7 million inhabitants. 80 percent of this increase is due to immigration, particularly that coming from EU/EFTA states<sup>1</sup>. Immigration brings benefits to the economy, society, and social works. However, rapid demographic growth also entails new challenges.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Economy and society</div>
<div>
The number of people immigrating to our country depends mainly on the labor market. In times of economic prosperity, companies do not find enough workforce in Switzerland and also hire personnel from abroad. Thanks to the free movement of persons, companies in the private sector, but also those in the public sector such as hospitals and nursing homes, can easily and quickly hire foreign labor coming from the EU/EFTA area. This possibility also has significant economic repercussions. In fact, since 2002 Switzerland's economic output has increased by 24 percent per inhabitant. The free movement of persons has also contributed to this result<sup>2</sup>.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Social insurances</div>
<div>
Workers coming from abroad provide a contribution to the financing of social insurances that is overall higher compared to the benefits they receive. Although in the unemployment insurance sector they collect benefits higher than the contributions they pay, as far as the AHV/OASI, IV/DI, and the income loss compensation are concerned, their contributions instead far exceed the benefits obtained<sup>3</sup>.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Need for foreign labor</div>
<div>
In the future as well, Swiss companies and public structures such as hospitals and nursing homes will depend on foreign labor to be able to fill vacant positions. Already today, the number of people retiring is higher than that of the new recruits<sup>4</sup>. Added to this is the fact that the number of elderly people is increasing and with it the need for care<sup>5</sup>.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">The challenges linked to population growth</div>
<div>
Population growth increases the demand for housing. To counter this pressure, the Federal Council promotes the construction of affordable housing. It also addresses the challenges in the areas of spatial planning, the environment, energy, and transport with numerous measures, for example by upgrading the road and railway network or strengthening environmental protection.
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">The requests of the initiative<br><br>Up to 2050 a maximum of ten million inhabitants</div>
<div>
The initiative «No to a 10 million Switzerland! (Sustainability initiative)» asks to limit the permanent resident population in Switzerland so that it does not exceed ten million inhabitants before 2050. The promoters of the initiative want to limit immigration to protect the environment and to sustainably conserve the natural foundations of life, but also to preserve the efficiency of infrastructure, healthcare, and social insurances. If the initiative is accepted, the Confederation and the Cantons will be required to adopt measures, particularly if certain limit values are exceeded.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Measures starting from 9.5 million people</div>
<div>
If before 2050 the permanent resident population in Switzerland were to exceed 9.5 million inhabitants, the initiative provides that:
<ul>
<li>the Federal Council and Parliament must intervene in particular in the asylum and family reunification sector. The initiative does not specify what other provisions to adopt;</li>
<li>provisionally admitted persons can no longer obtain a residence or settlement permit nor other rights to stay, and are furthermore excluded from the possibility of acquiring Swiss citizenship;</li>
<li>the Federal Council must invoke the exception or safeguard clauses provided for by international agreements that contribute to demographic growth or negotiate new clauses of this kind, or renegotiate the agreements in question. These could include, for example, the Agreement with the European Union on the free movement of persons, the Geneva Convention on Refugees, or the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child.</li>
</ul>
<p>According to the Confederation's projections, the 9.5 million limit will likely be exceeded in 2031<sup>6</sup>.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Measures starting from ten million people</div>
<div>
If the permanent resident population were to exceed ten million inhabitants, the initiative provides that:
<ul>
<li>the Federal Council and Parliament must adopt all provisions at their disposal so that the number of inhabitants returns to being less than ten million;</li>
<li>international agreements that contribute to demographic growth must be denounced as soon as possible.</li>
</ul>
<p>Starting from 2050, the Federal Council may adjust the limit of ten million to the extent of the population increase due to a possible surplus of births.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Denunciation of the free movement of persons</div>
<div>
If two years after the first exceeding of the threshold of ten million inhabitants, the permanent resident population should continue to stand above this limit, the Agreement with the European Union on the free movement of persons must be denounced. Switzerland could waive the denunciation if within this same timeframe it is possible to negotiate or invoke exception or safeguard clauses that allow ensuring compliance with the limit of ten million.
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 40px; padding-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color);">
<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Consequences of the initiative<br><br>Limitations on the labor market</div>
<div>
If Switzerland were to limit the number of inhabitants to ten million, it would be difficult for companies in the private sector and those in the public sector to continue to hire the necessary workforce. The Swiss economy and public structures such as hospitals and nursing homes would lack personnel. Denouncing the Agreement on the free movement of persons would make it more difficult to hire personnel from the EU/EFTA area. It would also be more difficult for Swiss citizens to work in those Countries.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">End of the Bilateral Agreements</div>
<div>
The Agreement on the free movement of persons is linked to the other Bilateral I Agreements: in the event of the denunciation of the first, even these latter would automatically lapse. These Agreements concern the reduction of technical barriers to trade, land and air transport, agriculture, and public procurement. For companies, but also for farmers, trade with the European Union would become more expensive and complicated. Studies show that the end of the Bilateral I Agreements would cause the Swiss economy revenue losses in the order of billions of francs, and the population would have less money at their disposal<sup>7</sup>.
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">End of the Schengen/Dublin Agreements</div>
<div>
The denunciation of the free movement of persons would also call into question Switzerland's participation in the Schengen and Dublin Agreements under the aegis of the European Union. Their end would have serious repercussions for the asylum sector and for Switzerland's internal security:
<ul>
<li>unlike today, Switzerland would have to carry out asylum procedures even for applicants who have already sought asylum in the European Union and could no longer transfer these people to other European States. Asylum seekers whose application has been rejected by a European Union State could also submit a new application in Switzerland. For this reason, the number of asylum procedures in our country would probably increase;</li>
<li>the Swiss police and border protection authorities would no longer have access to European databases regarding security and the search for persons, which today are fundamental tools for the internal security of the Country.</li>
</ul>
<p>In the asylum sector and in that of border protection as well as internal security, greater resources and supplementary expenses in the order of several hundreds of millions of francs per year would have to be foreseen<sup>8</sup>.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="grid-2-col">
<div class="side-label">Weakening of human rights</div>
<div>
If it were to renegotiate or denounce agreements concerning the protection of human rights, Switzerland would risk compromising its humanitarian tradition and finding itself isolated on the international level with potential negative consequences for the international reputation and credibility of our Country.
</div>
</div>
</div>

<h2 style="border:none; margin-bottom: 0;">Arguments</h2>

<!-- Initiative Committee (PRO) -->
<div class="arg-title pro">Initiative Committee</div>
<p style="font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 25px; font-size: 1.1em;">The consequences of mass immigration are under the eyes of all: shortage of housing and ever higher rents. Cementing of the landscape. Congested traffic and overcrowded trains. Criminality on the rise. The healthcare system is at the limit. In schools, the level of education decreases. Many feel increasingly foreigners in their own Country.</p>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Excessive immigration</div>
<div>
At the end of 2025, Switzerland counted approximately 9.1 million inhabitants. In 12 years, the permanent resident population has increased by 1 million people. The main cause is mass immigration. Over 100,000 additional people arrive in Switzerland every year, from the EU, from third Countries or through the asylum system. Many come from foreign Islamic cultures and largely depend on social assistance from the very first moment they arrive. Every day we read news of thefts, violent crimes and stabbings.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Shortage of housing</div>
<div>
Rents are in constant increase. The major part of Swiss people can no longer afford to buy a home, and this despite the fact that today much more is being built compared to the past. Mass immigration is the main cause of the shortage of housing.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Nature and agriculture under pressure</div>
<div>
Also our landscape changes face. Our beautiful Switzerland comes cemented day after day. Green areas and meadows disappear. Construction is increasingly dense and increasingly tall. Nature and ecology suffer from it. The quality of life is affected.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Schools in difficulty</div>
<div>
Switzerland has always been proud of its educational system. Today however schools are under pressure. Studies demonstrate it: the more foreign-language children there are in a class, the more the average level drops. A quarter of young Swiss, at the end of compulsory schooling, is not able to read correctly.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Immigration exacerbates the shortage of specialists</div>
<div>
Immigration creates even more immigration. For every 100,000 new immigrants approximately 45,000 supplementary apartments and about 2,200 more doctors and nurses are needed every year. This spiral without end is unsustainable.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">No to a 10 million Switzerland</div>
<div>
The sustainability initiative guarantees a reasonable permanent immigration: up to 2050 the permanent resident population will not be able to exceed the limit of 10 million. This means that approximately 40,000 people, in particular qualified workers, could still immigrate, every year.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Yes to a sustainable development</div>
<div>
<p>The initiative obliges politics to limit immigration to Switzerland in a reasonable manner:</p>
<p>Yes to a sustainable demographic development instead of an immigration without limits.</p>
<p>Yes to the protection of nature and the landscape.</p>
<p>Yes to the popular initiative «No to a 10 million Switzerland! (Sustainability initiative)».</p>
</div>
</div>

<div style="margin-top: 60px; padding-top: 40px; border-top: 2px dashed var(--border-color);">
<!-- Federal Council and Parliament (CON) -->
<div class="arg-title con">Federal Council and Parliament</div>
<p style="font-weight: bold; margin-bottom: 25px; font-size: 1.1em;">The initiative puts in danger well-being, the functioning of society, internal security and the humanitarian tradition of Switzerland. In times of political instability our Country needs reliable partnerships. For this reason the Federal Council wants to carry forward the proven bilateral path with the European Union. To the population increase it reacts with targeted measures. The Federal Council and Parliament reject the initiative in particular for the reasons set out below.</p>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Less well-being</div>
<div>
The initiative harms well-being. Swiss enterprises depend on foreign labor: if the permanent resident population were limited, enterprises could only partially assume personnel coming from the European Union. The economy and the well-being of Switzerland would resent it.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Social problems</div>
<div>
The initiative puts the functioning of society in danger. Hospitals and nursing homes could no longer assist and care for patients and elderly people with the usual quality. Also other sectors, as for example construction, would find themselves confronted with great problems, with consequent prejudice to infrastructures.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Uncertain relations with the European Union</div>
<div>
The initiative puts the bilateral path with the European Union in danger in a period already characterized by uncertainties. The Federal Council considers this prospect very risky. The European Union is the most important political and economic partner of our Country. For this reason the Federal Council wants to carry forward the bilateral path and assure our long-term well-being.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Less security</div>
<div>
The initiative would make the fight against criminality and terrorism more difficult. If the cooperation in the ambit of Schengen were to fail, Switzerland would not in fact have any more access to the European databases, fundamental for the protection of the population.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">More asylum requests</div>
<div>
If the Dublin Agreement were no longer to apply, Switzerland would receive probably a major number of asylum requests. Asylum seekers rejected by a European Union State could present a new application in Switzerland. In the sectors of asylum and internal security the Confederation and the Cantons should bear supplementary costs in the order of various hundreds of millions of francs per year.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">A danger for the humanitarian tradition</div>
<div>
The initiative puts in danger our humanitarian tradition. If Switzerland could no longer respect agreements for the protection of human rights as it has up to now, it would remain isolated on the international plane and would lose its own credibility.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">The initiative creates problems instead of providing solutions</div>
<div>
The initiative creates problems and does not provide solutions. The Federal Council, instead, faces the challenges tied to the population growth. It intends to exploit better the national potential of labor, promote the construction of housing at accessible prices and strongly reduce the number of asylum applications. In the package of Agreements between Switzerland and the European Union (Bilaterals III) it has moreover negotiated a safeguard clause that permits to temporarily limit immigration in case of grave problems.
</div>
</div>

<div>
For all these reasons, the Federal Council and Parliament recommend rejecting the popular initiative «No to a 10 million Switzerland! (Sustainability initiative)».
