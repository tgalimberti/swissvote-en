<h2>Subject of the vote</h2>

<p>The popular initiative "Yes to the neutralization of the increase in real estate valuations" gathered 17,352 valid signatures and asks for the introduction of a constitutional article (34 quater) according to which "the recurring general revision of real estate valuations cannot result overall in an automatic increase in the revenue of public taxes, nor an automatic reduction in services, aid, and subsidies provided in the context of pursuing social objectives". Furthermore, a transitional provision is also foreseen according to which this article "enters into force simultaneously with the new application law".</p>

<p>With the adoption of the new constitutional article, a principle would be anchored in the Constitution according to which the general revision of valuations, planned for 2035, must be accompanied by an examination of the impact of the variation in valuation values with respect to all the legal bases that refer to it, and by an evaluation of possible adjustments to sectoral laws. The objective of the initiative is to avoid an automatic mechanism whereby an increase in valuations linked to a general revision automatically corresponds, therefore without possible corrective measures being evaluated, to an increase in the tax burden and a reduction in social benefits. It should be noted that for some of these social benefits, the cantonal room for maneuver to introduce correctives is extremely limited, if not absent, since the rules for calculating wealth are anchored in federal legal bases.</p>

<p>Finally, it is pointed out that the expression "overall" contained in the text of the initiative is to be understood in relation to the total amounts, therefore disregarding the impact that the general revision of valuation values will have on individual specific cases. The evaluation of the consequences of the revision of real estate valuations cannot therefore concern those for the individual citizen, but only those inherent to the taxpayers or beneficiaries of social benefits as a whole.</p>
</div>

<h2 style="border:none; margin-bottom: 0;">Arguments</h2>

<!-- Initiative Promoters (PRO) -->
<div class="arg-title pro">Why vote YES</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Stop the hidden sting</div>
<div>
We are not talking about theory here. We are talking about 430 million francs a year. Your money. The initiative "Yes to the neutralization of the increase in valuation values" says a very simple thing: if the State updates real estate valuations, it cannot automatically increase taxes and cannot automatically cut subsidies. Period.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">The problem is real, and it is enormous</div>
<div>
The Council of State says it clearly in message 8646 of January 28, 2026: the real value of real estate is about 2.3 times the current valuation. Translated into more understandable words? The new valuations could more than double! Without compensation this means:
<ul>
<li>405 million francs more in taxes, every year;</li>
<li>26 million francs less in social benefits, every year.</li>
</ul>
This is why in total the revision of valuations without accompanying measures would weigh over 430 million francs on the shoulders of Ticino residents. Every year!
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">What does it mean concretely for all of us?</div>
<div>
The revision of valuations without compensatory measures means that:
<ul>
<li>a family pays more than double in wealth taxes for the same house;</li>
<li>an elderly person could lose their health insurance subsidy;</li>
<li>nursing home fees increase;</li>
<li>scholarships are lost;</li>
<li>study allowances are lost;</li>
<li>supplementary benefits are lost.</li>
</ul>
As you can see, it is not a simple question for "rich real estate owners". As many as 32 cantonal laws depend on the valuation value and are affected. The reform without correctives hits cities, villages, valleys. In short, it hits everyone. Even tenants, because such strong tax increases on owners would inevitably raise rents.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Protection of a series of groups of people</div>
<div>
Who do we protect with a yes at the ballot box:
<ul>
<li>the elderly person who lives in the house built over a lifetime of sacrifices;</li>
<li>the family that has inherited a property, perhaps even remote and to be renovated;</li>
<li>tenants, because the increased costs are passed on to rents;</li>
<li>Ticino businesses, hit by the real estate tax.</li>
</ul>
This is real protection. Not slogans.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Why amend the Constitution?</div>
<div>
Because the revision of valuations is in itself an administrative act. It does not pass through Parliament and it does not even pass by the People. The Government decides. That's it. This is why a strong constitutional protection is needed. We do not want automatic tax increases and social benefit cuts. The key word is: automatic. The initiative asks that, if valuations increase, the compensations must be triggered immediately: all together; without tricks; without postponements; without appeals used to block only the compensations while letting the reform continue. Writing it in the Constitution means protecting those who are vulnerable from the revision today, tomorrow, and for all future revisions: if anyone wants to change this principle, they will have to go back before the people.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Nothing is blocked, only abuses</div>
<div>
The initiative does not prevent technical updates; it does not freeze the system; it does not paralyze politics. The initiative establishes only a rule of common sense: a technical revision cannot become a cash machine.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">It is a question of equity</div>
<div>
The State cannot double the valuation of a house by taxing the owner more than double or suddenly denying them state benefits, when in reality the owner has not earned a single extra franc. The State cannot use an administrative operation to put its hands in the citizens' pockets. The State cannot take subsidies away from those who need them, just for a technical calculation.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">On June 14 we ask you for a decisive Yes at the ballot box</div>
<div>
We ask you, your relatives, friends and acquaintances to vote and to convincingly get others to vote Yes to a very simple principle: we want to protect ourselves from a bloodletting of over 430 million francs a year that could be withdrawn through a simple administrative modification. An increase that would not pass through Parliament and on which the people could not vote.<br><br>Vote yes. For equity. For stability. To defend families, the elderly, tenants, and businesses. Because 430 million francs a year is not a detail. It is a sting. And we say: enough.
</div>
</div>

<!-- Opposing Committee (CON) -->
<div class="arg-title con">Why vote NO</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">The Constitution is the basis of our coexistence</div>
<div>
The Constitution serves to protect the freedom and dignity of every person and to place clear limits on power. For this reason it contains general principles and rules of the game valid over time: fundamental rights, the separation of powers, the organization of the State.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Constitutional popular initiative</div>
<div>
In Switzerland we have another precious tool: the constitutional popular initiative. Since 1891 it allows citizens to participate directly in writing the Constitution. It is a pillar of our direct democracy and must be defended. But defending the constitutional popular initiative does not mean approving just any proposal. The Constitution must remain clear, coherent, and stable. Inserting norms linked to specific issues or particular interests risks weakening it.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">What the initiative provides</div>
<div>
The initiative says that, when real estate valuations are updated, they must not automatically entail either an overall increase in tax revenue or an automatic reduction in social benefits.<br>
Translated into simple words: if the valuations were to rise, the Parliament should intervene to prevent the Canton from collecting more or some benefits from being reduced automatically.<br>
At first glance it may seem like a common-sense measure. In reality it asks for something that already happens today.<br>
The revision of valuations must in any case pass through the Grand Council. The Parliament already has the competence, contextually, to modify rates, exemptions, and related laws. It can decide to compensate the effects or to maintain them. Even choosing to modify nothing is a political decision. The legislator always exercises its role.<br>
The initiative therefore introduces a procedural obligation into the Constitution that de facto already exists. It adds no new rights, it does not strengthen fundamental principles, but it inserts a technical detail into a text that should contain long-term rules.<br>
Our taxation is already founded on clear constitutional principles: equality, equal treatment, and taxation according to economic capacity. The Federal Supreme Court oversees their respect and these elements anchored to the Constitution are sufficient for the legislator.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Real estate valuations – equal treatment</div>
<div>
Today in Ticino real estate valuations are stuck at 40% of the market value, while federal jurisprudence indicates that for a question of equal treatment the threshold should be situated around 70%. The revision will therefore be necessary to bring the system back within more correct and legal parameters.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Revision of valuations</div>
<div>
The revision, previously planned for 2025, has been postponed to 2035. The promoters of the initiative continue to support its necessity using the bogeyman of the tax increase, an assessment that will in any case be up to the Parliament, and ultimately the citizens, to make. It must be said that this postponement entails an estimated renunciation of about 400 million francs in revenue for the Canton in the coming years. At a time when public finances are under pressure, it is a fact that cannot be ignored. Consider also that this money would be necessary to mitigate the effect of health insurance premiums on family budgets.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Cases of elderly owners of primary residences</div>
<div>
In the debate, cases are often cited of elderly owners of a primary house with a low mortgage, but with modest incomes. These are real situations that deserve attention. However, there are targeted tools to support them, for example intervening on wealth exemption thresholds or with specific social measures. Blocking or stiffening the revision of valuations in the Constitution is not the most effective solution. Implicitly asking for a neutralization of the adjustment of valuations only favors large capitals and takes resources away from intervening on the true needs of citizens, owners and non-owners alike.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Effects of the revision</div>
<div>
In summary, the supporters of the initiative state that Parliament will have to evaluate the effects of the revision and adopt possible correctives. But this task already belongs to the legislator today. Inserting it into the Constitution does not change the substance, while it risks transforming an ordinary political choice into a rigid and potentially problematic constraint in the future.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point">
<div class="arg-point-title">Instrument of balance</div>
<div>
The Constitution must remain an instrument of balance above contingencies, not a container of particular norms in favor of one or the other. Concrete tax decisions must be able to be discussed and adapted through laws, with the normal democratic confrontation.
</div>
</div>

<div class="arg-point" style="margin-top: 30px; border-top: 1px solid var(--border-color); padding-top: 20px;">
<div class="arg-point-title">Recommendation against the initiative</div>
<div>
For all these reasons, it is recommended to vote <strong style="color: var(--swiss-red);">NO</strong> to the elaborated constitutional popular initiative.
</div>
</div>
